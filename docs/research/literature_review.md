## 1. How the field does it

### 1.1 The ObjectNav task: definitions, metrics, and the state of the leaderboard

The modern study of "find an object in an unseen building" rests on two definitional papers. Anderson et al. (2018) laid down the ground rules that still govern the field: agents must be evaluated in environments unseen during training; episodes end only when the agent deliberately signals completion with a STOP action (oracle-stopping inflates apparent competence); and success alone is insufficient, so the primary metric is SPL — Success weighted by Path Length, SPL = (1/N) Σᵢ Sᵢ·lᵢ/max(pᵢ, lᵢ), where lᵢ is the *geodesic* (around-obstacle) shortest-path distance and pᵢ the agent's actual path. Batra et al. (2020) then specialized this to Object-Goal Navigation: success requires calling STOP within a 1.0 m Euclidean shell of any instance of the target category with the object oracle-visible from the stopping pose, using the canonical discrete action space (MOVE_FORWARD 0.25 m, 30° turns/tilts), with SPL computed to the instance closest to the start. The Habitat Challenges (Yadav et al., 2022–2023) froze these choices into executable configs: HM3D-Semantics v0.1 with 120 scenes split 80/20/20, six goal categories, a 500-step budget, RGB-D plus noiseless GPS+Compass, and the metric quartet Success/SPL/SoftSPL/distance-to-goal. SoftSPL (Datta et al., 2020) replaces binary success with soft progress, max(0, 1 − d_T/d_init), making partial progress measurable when success is rare.

The leaderboard arc on HM3D val tells a consistent story. End-to-end RL from scratch was essentially at zero SPL in the MP3D era. Imitation-plus-RL (PIRLNav; Ramrakhya et al., 2023) advanced the state of the art from 60.0% to 65.0% success, with its released RL-finetuned checkpoint at 70.4 success / 34.1 SPL (evaluation split unlabeled in the release). Exploitation-guided modular training (XGX; Wasserman et al., 2023) pushed success to roughly 73% (the frequently quoted 72.9% figure is not independently verified; the official repo reproduces 0.7275 success / 0.3613 SPL). Meanwhile zero-shot systems approach trained performance with no navigation training at all: VLFM (Yokoyama et al., 2024) at 52.5 success / 30.4 SPL and SG-Nav (Yin et al., 2024) at 54.0 / 24.9 (GPT variant). The persistent signature across all of it: SPL lags success by 25–40 points. Agents find objects, but they wander badly doing so — exactly the gap WanderAI's SPL-versus-oracle framing probes.

### 1.2 End-to-end RL: sample complexity and the generalization wall

DD-PPO (Wijmans et al., 2020) is the maximalist proof: with a dense geodesic-progress reward, GPS+Compass, and near-linear distributed scaling (107× speedup on 128 GPUs), model-free RL "essentially solves" PointNav — 2.5 billion frames ("80 years of human experience") yield near-perfect navigation in unseen scenes (Gibson val SPL up to 0.969 for depth models; a blind agent reaches 0.729). Error versus computation follows a power law: 90% of peak performance arrives at 100M steps, and the last few percent cost the remaining 25×. VER (Wijmans et al., 2022) is the systems continuation, making on-policy RL 1.6–2.7× faster so billions of frames stay affordable.

Pointed at ObjectNav, the same recipe collapsed. General-purpose CNN+RNN models managed ~6% success on MP3D, and THDA (Maksymets et al., 2021) diagnosed the mechanism with the cleanest published number for the generalization gap: 94% success on training scenes versus 8% on validation — layout memorization instead of learned exploration, encouraged by finite scene sets and strong encoders. The fixes all amount to importing priors from outside RL: auxiliary tasks plus exploration rewards (Ye et al., 2021: 24.5% success / 8.1% SPL on MP3D, a 37%/8% relative improvement); layout augmentation (THDA: 14%→27% success, 7.5→11.1 SPL); human demonstrations (Habitat-Web; Ramrakhya et al., 2022: IL on 70k human demos beats RL on 240k agent trajectories by +3.3 success); offline visual pretraining (OVRL; Yadav et al., 2022: ObjectNav 18.1%→23.2%, ImageNav 29.2%→54.2%; OVRL-v2, 2023: 64.0% ObjectNav success on HM3D with a modules-free ViT+LSTM); and IL-then-RL finetuning (PIRLNav). PIRLNav's scaling analysis closes the loop: as the pretrained prior strengthens, RL's marginal gain shrinks — 90% of its best performance is reachable with less than half the demonstrations. By 2023 the field's leverage had moved from RL algorithms to the quality of the prior being finetuned — precisely the bet WanderAI makes with a language-pretrained policy.

### 1.3 Modular map-based methods

Active Neural SLAM (Chaplot et al., 2020a) set the template every later system inherits: a mapping module projecting egocentric observations into an allocentric top-down grid with pose correction, a global policy selecting a long-term (x, y) goal at coarse timescale, and an analytic local planner. On Gibson val exploration it reached 0.948 relative coverage versus 0.789 for the best baseline, and its zero-shot PointNav transfer won the CVPR 2019 Habitat challenge. SemExp (Chaplot et al., 2020b) made the map semantic (Mask R-CNN channels) and the global policy goal-conditioned, winning the CVPR 2020 ObjectNav challenge (25.3% vs 18.8% for second place) and scoring 0.657 success / 0.339 SPL on Gibson val. Occupancy Anticipation (Ramakrishnan et al., 2020) upgraded the mapper to predict occupancy beyond the visible rays, winning the 2020 PointNav challenge.

The strand's later trajectory is a lesson in humility for learned exploration. PONI (Ramakrishnan et al., 2022) replaced the RL-trained global policy with a *supervised, interaction-free* frontier potential-function network and beat it: 73.6% success / 41.0% SPL on Gibson val (+3.7 success / +5.3 SPL over the best non-interactive prior; note the paper's SPL claim also holds on MP3D at 31.8/12.1, though the interactive Red-Rabbit method scored higher MP3D success), with up to 1,600× less training compute. Stubborn (Luo et al., 2022) went further: a deliberately learning-free agent — deterministic multi-stage sweep, untrapping logic, multi-frame detection aggregation — placed second in the 2021 challenge at 23.7% success / 0.098 SPL on MP3D test-standard (a separate local verification run reports 0.233/0.139 with 4.301 DTG; the two evaluations should not be conflated), and later papers (PEANUT) note its MP3D val success remained matched only by learned methods. The strand's converged lesson: given a decent metric map and planner, mapping fidelity, stuck-recovery, and detection reliability contribute more than exploration-policy sophistication.

### 1.4 Frontier-based exploration: from Yamauchi to semantic frontier scoring

Frontier-based exploration (Yamauchi, 1997) is the most durable algorithmic idea in this literature: classify occupancy-grid cells as open/occupied/unknown, define frontiers as boundaries between open and unknown space, cluster and size-filter them, and navigate to the nearest accessible frontier. Holz et al. (2010) provide the standard taxonomy of frontier *selection* — cost-based (distance), utility-based (expected information), and cost-utility hybrids — with the sobering finding that nearest-frontier is a strong, hard-to-beat default in indoor spaces.

The zero-shot ObjectNav wave reused this machinery wholesale and moved all innovation into semantic frontier scoring. CoW (Gadre et al., 2023) made plain FBE plus an open-vocabulary detector the canonical training-free baseline, matching a ZSON policy trained for 500M steps on MP3D navigation efficiency (the specific 4.9 vs 4.8 SPL pairing is not independently verified at a primary source). ESC (Zhou et al., 2023) scored frontiers with GLIP-grounded semantic maps plus LLM commonsense via probabilistic soft logic: 28.7 SR / 14.2 SPL on MP3D — a 288% relative SR gain over CoW — and 39.2/22.3 on HM3D. VLFM (Yokoyama et al., 2024) replaced text reasoning with a BLIP-2 image-text-similarity value map over frontiers: HM3D 52.5/30.4, MP3D 36.4/17.5, Gibson 84.0/52.2, state-of-the-art zero-shot SPL on all three (+13.3 SR / +8.1 SPL over ESC on HM3D). ApexNav (Zhang et al., 2025) added adaptive switching between semantic and geometric frontier modes plus multi-frame target-evidence fusion: HM3D-v1 59.6/33.0, HM3D-v2 76.2/38.0.

Gervet et al. (2023) explain why this modular scaffolding dominates in practice: in six real homes, modular learned exploration hit 90% success and classical FBE 80%, while end-to-end RL collapsed from 77% in sim to 23% in reality — the image domain gap. The through-line: frontier *detection* is a solved commodity; frontier *selection* is where all headroom lives.

### 1.5 Zero-shot LLM/VLM-guided navigation — and its skeptical re-evaluation

In every zero-shot LLM-navigation system, the language model is frozen and spatial competence is outsourced to engineered geometry. The LLM's role evolved through four forms: none (CoW); commonsense object-room priors constraining frontier choice (ESC; L3MVN, Yu et al., 2023: an LLM scores frontiers by nearby-object context, reaching 76.1 SR / 37.7 SPL on Gibson and 50.4/23.1 on HM3D); LLM-as-search-heuristic (LFG; Shah et al., 2023: poll the LLM with positive and negative chain-of-thought prompts and use vote frequencies as a planner heuristic — crucially, their GreedyLLM arm, an LLM used directly as the planner, fails outright); and dense structured representations (InstructNav, Long et al., 2024: LLM-planned action-landmark chains grounded through multi-sourced value maps, 58.0 SR but only 20.9 SPL on HM3D-v1; SG-Nav, Yin et al., 2024: hierarchical 3D scene graphs serialized into LLM prompts, 54.0/24.9 on HM3D).

Two caveats matter for citation hygiene. First, SG-Nav's abstract claims of ">10% SR" gains over prior zero-shot methods refer to relative gains or older baselines; its own results table shows absolute margins of +1.5 to +3.4 SR over the strongest prior zero-shot method. Second, the strand shows a consistent signature: frozen-LLM planners raise SR but depress SPL (InstructNav), while geometry-heavy value maps raise SPL (VLFM). The sharpest recent result radicalizes this: Aghaei et al. (2025, "When Engineering Outruns Intelligence") strip InstructNav's language components and replace them with a distance-weighted geometric frontier score, *improving* success from 58.0% to 61.1% and SPL from 20.9 to 36.0 on the full HM3D-v1 val set with zero API calls; adding a GPT-4.1 semantic vote yields only a modest further boost (the precise ~2%/200-episode figure is not independently verified). In the frozen-LLM regime, the LLM's marginal contribution over good frontier geometry is small.

### 1.6 Fine-tuned LLM/VLM policies and reinforcement fine-tuning of agents

This strand splits into two waves. Wave 1 (2023–2024) adapts LLMs/VLMs to embodied tasks with *supervised* learning: NaviLLM (Zheng et al., 2024) serializes navigation into schema-based text prompts and multi-task finetunes Vicuna-7B (CVDN goal progress 6.16, +29% over prior SOTA; R2R 67 SR / 59 SPL); NavGPT-2 (Zhou et al., 2024) keeps a FlanT5 frozen and trains a topological-graph policy head (R2R val-unseen 73.82 SR / 61.06 SPL); EmbodiedGPT (Mu et al., 2023) prefix-tunes a 7B LM on egocentric chain-of-thought plans; OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) fine-tunes a 7B VLM on 970K robot trajectories to emit actions as tokens, outperforming RT-2-X (55B) by 16.5% absolute success with 7× fewer parameters. None optimizes an environment reward.

Wave 2 (2024–2026) is reinforcement fine-tuning of the language model itself in multi-turn environments, trending toward small open models and critic-free group-based methods. ETO (Song et al., 2024) iterates trajectory-level DPO on failure-success pairs (+22% over SFT on out-of-distribution ScienceWorld). ArCHer (Zhou et al., 2024) shows hierarchical actor-critic with an utterance-level TD critic yields ~100× sample-efficiency over token-level PPO — the sharpest published critique of whole-episode credit assignment. GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025) extends GRPO with episode-level groups plus step-level anchor-state advantages, reaching 86.7% ALFWorld success with Qwen2.5-1.5B and 90.8% with 7B, with abstract-level gains of >12% on ALFWorld and >9% on WebShop over vanilla GRPO (per-model-size breakdowns are not independently verified). Closest of all to WanderAI: VLN-R1 (Qi et al., 2025) GRPO-finetunes Qwen2-VL-2B/7B to emit low-level forward/turn/stop actions in continuous VLN-CE; after RFT, the 2B model matches or surpasses the 7B SFT-only model, and 10K RFT samples beat full-corpus SFT for cross-domain transfer — but its time-decayed reward still scores alignment with expert reference trajectories, i.e., imitation-flavored RL requiring demonstrations. Reward signals across Wave 2 are almost always sparse task success or expert alignment; dense geometry-derived shaping is conspicuously rare.

### 1.7 Reward design for goal-reaching navigation

The theory is settled: Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) proved that adding F(s,a,s′) = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) for any potential Φ is necessary and sufficient for preserving optimal policies, with Φ ≈ V* making the shaped problem maximally easy. Embodied AI operationalized exactly this with privileged simulator state: Habitat's NavRLEnv (Savva et al., 2019) defined r_t = slack + (d_{t−1} − d_t) + R·1[success] with d the *geodesic* distance (slack −0.01; success bonus 10.0 in the 2019 code, standardized to 2.5 by DD-PPO). Geodesic rather than Euclidean matters because −d_geo is proportional to the optimal value function under unit step cost, so its delta never rewards walking into a wall. Notably, the standard recipe contains no collision penalty (Habitat defaults to wall-sliding); explicit collision costs appear in exploration-focused work (Chen et al., 2019: −0.006 per colliding step alongside a +0.0005-per-new-cell coverage reward).

When the goal's location is unknown, optimal pre-detection behavior is information-gathering, and distance shaping is initially uninformative — hence intrinsic bonuses: prediction-error curiosity (ICM; Pathak et al., 2017), random-network distillation (RND; Burda et al., 2019, with its warning that intrinsic/extrinsic mixing is fragile enough to need separate value heads), episodic reachability novelty (Savinov et al., 2019 — whose "Grid Oracle" baseline, a bonus for visiting new spatial-grid cells, is essentially WanderAI's visited-cells signal, treated there as privileged), coverage deltas (Chen et al., 2019), and attenuated visit-count bonuses on top of the standard geodesic reward (Ye et al., 2021: 0.25/n(cell), attenuation 0.99). The engineering lessons recur: keep the bonus small relative to the success reward, attenuate it, and measure novelty on a task-relevant spatial abstraction.

### 1.8 Memory and mapping under bounded budgets

Four families occupy distinct points on a memory-versus-capability curve. Metric occupancy maps (log-odds Bayesian grids; OctoMap, Hornung et al., 2013) bound per-cell state via log-odds clamping, enabling octree pruning that keeps building-scale maps small; memory scales with explored area, not trajectory length. Topological memories (SPTM; Savinov et al., 2018: a reachability-network graph over walkthrough frames, tripling goal-reaching success over the best baseline in unseen VizDoom mazes; VGM, Kwon et al., 2021: online similarity-gated node insertion, 0.71 SR / 0.48 SPL overall on Gibson image-nav) grow with experience. Transformer episodic memories (SMT; Fang et al., 2019) store every embedded observation with O(T) memory and quadratic attention; its memory-capacity ablation (50–500 slots) is the clearest published memory-performance curve. Implicit neural maps (RNR-Map; Kwon et al., 2023: a renderable latent code per grid cell, 65.7% success on NRNS-curved) invert the compression entirely.

The bounded-memory line is thinner and more recent, and its consistent finding is that principled forgetting *improves* navigation. SGM (Emmons et al., 2020) merges topological nodes under two-way (goal/start interchangeability) consistency with provably linear path-length degradation, improving success 2.1× on average over demonstration-free baselines SoRB and SPTM (its 60.7% ViZDoom figure is below demonstration-using prior work by design, not a SOTA claim). MemoNav (Li et al., 2024) drops the bottom-20%-relevance memory nodes and gains +4.7 SR / +2.5 SPL over its own full-memory VGM baseline. The field, however, almost never reports memory in bytes alongside SPL — the explicit SPL-versus-memory-budget frontier is largely unmeasured.

### 1.9 Planar surface extraction and navigable-space estimation

Two lineages dominate. The geometric lineage runs from RANSAC through Schnabel et al. (2007), whose efficient multi-shape RANSAC — octree-localized sampling, a joint distance-plus-normal inlier test, a connectivity constraint, and a miss-probability stopping rule — decomposes multi-million-point clouds "within less than a minute" and whose parameterization became the de facto interface (reproduced in CGAL and modern robot stacks). For organized depth images, PEAC (Feng et al., 2014) merges 10×10-pixel blocks by plane-fit MSE at >35 Hz on 640×480, and CAPE (Proença & Gao, 2018) pushes cell-grid extraction to ~300 Hz on one CPU core — plane segmentation is essentially free next to per-step LLM inference. The learned lineage (PlaneRCNN, Liu et al., 2019; PlanarRecon, Xie et al., 2022: F-score 0.372 at 40 ms/keyframe on ScanNet versus 0.304 for an ESTDepth+PEAC classical pipeline) recovers planes from RGB alone.

Navigation stacks rarely consume raw plane lists. The dominant pattern is depth → robot-centric 2.5D elevation map (Miki et al., 2022: bounded 8 m × 8 m grid at 4 cm) → per-cell traversability from surface geometry: slope via acos(n_z) against a critical angle (45° default in Wermelinger et al., 2016), step-height thresholds, roughness. Grandia et al. (2023) give the sharpest recipe for "planes define where the robot may act": sliding-window plane extraction with two-threshold inclination criteria (30° patch / 35° cell versus world-z, ~2 cm planarity RMSE), refined with Schnabel's RANSAC, converted to convex regions constraining an MPC. This is the exact template for estimating, from first-person depth, the navigable-space grid WanderAI's reward oracle currently gets for free.

### 1.10 Procedural scenes and cross-scene generalization

Handcrafted interactive simulators plateaued at O(10–100) environments (AI2-iTHOR 120 scenes, RoboTHOR 89, iGibson 15; Kolve et al., 2017; Deitke et al., 2020), scanned datasets at O(100–1000) (MP3D, Gibson, HM3D at 1,000 scenes / 112.5k m² navigable space, 1.4–3.7× larger than predecessors; Ramakrishnan et al., 2021 — the per-dataset scene/area breakdowns are internally consistent with the verified abstract but not independently verified). HM3D-trained PointNav agents are Pareto-optimal across test sets, reaching 100% success on Gibson-test. ProcTHOR (Deitke et al., 2022) broke the scene-count ceiling with procedural generation — 10K interactive houses in an hour — with a monotonic scale ablation (RoboTHOR zero-shot success 8.53%→20.9%→33.1%→44.5% across 10/100/1K/10K training houses) and SOTA on six benchmarks. Conceptually this generalizes domain randomization (Tobin et al., 2017) from textures to whole layouts: make training variability wide enough and any test scene is "just another variation."

The counterweight is HSSD-200 (Khanna et al., 2024): agents trained on just 122 high-quality scenes beat agents trained on 10,000 ProcTHOR scenes at zero-shot ObjectNav on HM3D-Semantics val (19.15% vs 12.53% success) — "scale helps in generalization, but the benefits quickly saturate, making visual fidelity and correlation to real-world scenes more important." RoboTHOR's sim-to-real experiments reinforce the point: scene-scale-pretrained SOTA agents drop to ~33–35% success on real robots, driven largely by appearance shift. The synthesis: scene diversity is necessary and cheap; past O(100)–O(1K) scenes the binding constraint is distribution match, not count.

### 1.11 Text/symbolic-observation navigation and LLM spatial reasoning

Three camps share one interface decision — render the world to the LLM as text. Systems work textualizes perception for frozen LLMs: NavGPT (Zhou et al., 2023) verbalizes panoramic observations for zero-shot R2R (~34% SR, a ~40-point gap to trained agents); L3MVN verbalizes map frontiers. Learning work fine-tunes small LMs on text: LangNav (Pan et al., 2023) SFTs LLaMA2-7B on real plus 10k GPT-4-synthetic caption-trajectories, winning in low-data regimes (SPL 27–28 vs 6–11 for vision baselines at 10 shots) but reaching only ~43% SR with full data; GLAM (Carta et al., 2023) fine-tunes FLAN-T5 780M *online with PPO* in BabyAI-Text, drastically beating symbolic-PPO on sample efficiency and generalizing at 87–88% to unseen and nonsense-named objects (figures as reported by the authors; not independently re-verified in this corpus pass). ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2020) anchors the text-world-to-embodied lineage, with ReAct-prompted PaLM-540B reaching 71% on unseen tasks versus 37% for the trained BUTLER agent.

Probing work documents why frozen LLMs fail at the geometric layer. PPNL (Aghzal et al., 2023) and its follow-up show GPT-4 needs interleaved reason-act prompting for even short-horizon gridworld planning, fails long-horizon and optimal-path settings, and that a fine-tuned T5 reaches ~98% in-distribution while collapsing on new grid sizes (figures as reported; not independently re-verified). Yamada et al. (2023) show structure-dependent, path-length-sensitive breakdown of LLMs' implicit spatial maps. SpatialEval (Wang et al., 2024) shows spatial tasks can drop competitive models below random guessing, and — counter-intuitively — VLMs often underperform their text-only LLM counterparts, leaning on textual cues even when images are provided. The composite picture: prompting handles semantic decisions (which room, which frontier) but not metric ones (which turn, how far); only fine-tuning — best of all, online RL as in GLAM — has been shown to repair the metric layer.

### 1.12 Evaluation rigor and statistical practice

Two literatures converge on the checklist. The navigation side prescribes protocol: scene-disjoint splits with versioned episode datasets (~1000 held-out episodes is the challenge convention; Gibson PointNav val is 994 episodes); self-termination via STOP; exact success criteria and step budgets; and a metric suite — Success, SPL (primary; Anderson et al. calibrate "an SPL of 0.5" as good performance in unseen environments), SoftSPL, distance-to-goal, steps, plus visitation/coverage metrics at fixed step checkpoints for exploration claims (Ramakrishnan et al., 2021, IJCV). ProcTHOR contributes the out-of-generator evaluation pattern: ArchitecTHOR, a test-only artist-designed set, measures whether procedural training generalizes beyond the generator's own distribution.

The deep-RL side prescribes statistics. Henderson et al. (2018) showed two 5-seed groups of the identical algorithm can produce learning curves that appear to come from different distributions, and that surveyed papers used ≤5 seeds. Agarwal et al. (2021) prescribe the remedy, packaged in rliable: interquartile mean with 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals over the task-by-run matrix, performance profiles, and probability-of-improvement — usable with 3–10 runs, whereas defensible median comparisons required ~25 runs for a 25% improvement and ~100 for a 10% improvement in their Atari 100k case study. Even the Habitat Challenge organizers reserve the right to use additional metrics "in case of statistically insignificant SPL differences."

## 3. Where WanderAI stands

WanderAI is a minimal, fully instrumented instantiation of the standard ObjectNav protocol designed to isolate one question the SOTA literature leaves entangled: **can a small language model, RL-fine-tuned on episodic return, learn the exploration/exploitation policy itself from symbolic observations** — rather than delegating spatial reasoning to frontier planners or learning it from tens of thousands of human demonstrations?

**vs. the ObjectNav task strand.** WanderAI's action space (MOVE_FORWARD 0.25 m, TURN 30°) exactly matches the Batra et al. (2020) / Habitat 2022 standard and should be presented as deliberate alignment. It must adopt: the 500-step budget, the 1.0 m success shell, SPL computed with geodesic shortest-path length (its wavefront-Dijkstra module makes this free), and SoftSPL (a three-line addition given per-step geodesic distance). Honest deltas: the goal is a single fixed instance ("the red ball"), making it instance-goal-like and semantically easier; the symbolic observation oracle-solves perception; and there is currently **no STOP action**, violating the intentionality criterion every benchmark enforces — add one or explicitly flag the oracle-stop relaxation. Cite Anderson et al. (2018) for SPL, Batra et al. (2020) for the task, Datta et al. (2020) for SoftSPL.

**vs. end-to-end RL.** WanderAI inverts the trade: where DD-PPO spends 10⁸–10⁹ frames learning perception, mapping, memory, and exploration inside an RNN, WanderAI hard-codes those into the privileged symbolic observation and spends its budget only on decision-making, with the LLM prior playing the role human demos play in Habitat-Web and SSL pretraining plays in OVRL. It must report the train-vs-held-out gap as a headline table (THDA's 94%/8% makes this obligatory), and should cite PIRLNav for the pretrain-then-RL-finetune pattern — noting that PIRLNav's critic-instability finding directly motivates critic-free GRPO, and its diminishing-returns finding predicts GRPO gains will concentrate on grounding, not high-level strategy.

**vs. modular map methods and FBE.** These assume metric pose and dense allocentric grids; WanderAI gives the agent no coordinates and no map. What it must adopt: a Yamauchi-style nearest-frontier FBE baseline over its bounded-memory map (per Holz et al., nearest-frontier is the right default; largest/information-gain as ablations), and a Stubborn-style deterministic sweep as the honest heuristic floor — the literature shows learning-free agents frequently beat learned exploration. PONI's 1,600×-cheaper interaction-free frontier scorer is the efficiency yardstick WanderAI's RL must justify itself against. Cite ANS for the three-tier decomposition its mapping extension will miniaturize, SemExp as the most load-bearing task relative, OccAnt for the planned depth-to-navigable-space extension.

**vs. zero-shot LLM navigation.** Every system here keeps the LLM frozen as a frontier-scoring oracle; WanderAI's base-qwen3-4b baseline is the direct analogue of LFG's failing GreedyLLM-as-planner arm, and its fine-tuned model asks the question this strand structurally cannot: how much navigation can be trained *into* the LLM? Aghaei et al. (2025) is the decisive methodological warning: since pure frontier geometry matches frozen-LLM gains, WanderAI's FBE baseline must be reported alongside random and oracle, or any advantage is attributable to scaffolding. InstructNav's SR-high/SPL-low signature gives the concrete quantitative target that geodesic shaping directly optimizes.

**vs. fine-tuned LLM policies.** VLN-R1 is the nearest neighbor (GRPO RFT of a small VLM for egocentric low-level actions); the sharp difference is the reward — VLN-R1's time-decayed alignment to expert trajectories is imitation-flavored, whereas WanderAI's potential-based geodesic shaping is demonstration-free and computed from environment geometry. WanderAI should borrow GiGPO's identical-initial-state rollout groups and anchor-state step advantages (trivially implementable given discretized cells), an ETO-style DPO baseline minted from its geodesic oracle, and cite ArCHer honestly: dense per-step shaping partially substitutes for the turn-level critic ArCHer shows whole-episode GRPO lacks.

**vs. reward design.** WanderAI's reward r = α(d_prev − d_cur) − β_step − κ·collision + R·success is **not novel** — it is the Habitat/DD-PPO standard plus a collision penalty (precedent: Chen et al. 2019's −0.006). Cite Ng et al. (1999) Theorem 1 for policy invariance with Φ = −α·d_geo, and frame the genuine contribution surface: whether the standard reward transfers to the whole-episode-return GRPO regime, and observation-side (visited-cells in text) versus reward-side (Ye et al.'s count bonus) exploration as an explicit design axis.

**vs. memory/mapping and planar surfaces.** WanderAI sits simultaneously at the extreme-compression end of the metric-map family (one bit per cell, no coordinates exposed) and the bounded end of the transformer-memory family (LLM context = SMT-style episodic memory with a hard token budget) — a combination none of these papers study. Adopt: log-odds occupancy with clamping for the mapping module, SGM/MemoNav mechanisms as candidate forgetting policies, and SMT's methodology of publishing SPL-vs-memory-budget curves. For the depth extension, the recipe is fully specified in prior work: PEAC-style block clustering or 3-point RANSAC with Schnabel's joint inlier test, then the Wermelinger/Grandia normal-angle-to-gravity criterion, accumulated in a Miki-style bounded 2.5D grid — closing the loop on the privileged occupancy grid the reward oracle currently gets for free.

**vs. procedural scenes and evaluation rigor.** WanderAI's protocol is ProcTHOR in miniature, but with the appearance confound removed by construction — its N-room sweep is a clean measurement of layout-only scene scaling, where ProcTHOR predicts monotonic gains and HSSD-200 predicts early saturation. It needs an out-of-generator test set (the ArchitecTHOR analog). On statistics, it must escape the single-seed regime Henderson et al. discredited: ~1000 frozen, versioned held-out episodes; IQM success/SPL with 95% stratified bootstrap CIs (rliable applies directly); multiple eval replicates for decoding stochasticity; 3+ training seeds or an explicit limitation; probability-of-improvement over the FBE baseline rather than bare deltas.

## 4. Candidate contributions

**C1. Demonstration-free reinforcement fine-tuning of a small text-only LLM into a closed-loop, low-level object-search policy from purely symbolic egocentric observations, with generalization measured by SPL on held-out procedural scenes.** Must be differentiated from: VLN-R1 (GRPO RFT for low-level VLN-CE actions, but a VLM on video, instruction-following not search, and an imitation-flavored time-decayed reward requiring expert trajectories); GLAM (online RL of a 780M LM on symbolic text, but per-step PPO, discrete gridworld, sparse reward); LangNav (SFT, not RL). Required evidence: fine-tuned vs. base-LLM vs. FBE vs. random/oracle on frozen held-out episodes, with IQM + bootstrap CIs and a probability-of-improvement statement; an SFT/DPO baseline showing RL specifically (not just weight updating) is responsible.

**C2. Privileged geodesic potential-based shaping as a sufficient, demonstration-free training signal for LLM-agent RFT, partially substituting for turn-level credit assignment.** Differentiated from: DD-PPO/Habitat (same reward, but per-step TD on CNN policies — the reward itself is standard and must not be claimed); ArCHer (which argues a learned turn-level critic is needed); VLN-R1 and PIRLNav (which need demonstrations). Required evidence: ablation of sparse-success vs. shaped reward under identical GRPO budgets; comparison against a GiGPO-style step-level advantage variant; analysis showing the per-step shaping term carries the credit-assignment load.

**C3. Observation-side exploration memory as an alternative to reward-side exploration bonuses.** WanderAI renders visited-cells state into the observation and keeps the reward purely goal-directed; the literature's closest objects put the same signal in the reward (Ye et al.'s attenuated 0.25/n(cell) bonus; Savinov et al.'s Grid Oracle). Required evidence: a 2×2 ablation (memory in observation × count bonus in reward) measuring success, SPL, and coverage-vs-step curves; evidence the LLM actually conditions on the memory field (perturbation tests).

**C4. A confound-free scene-count scaling curve for layout-only generalization.** ProcTHOR shows monotonic in-distribution gains to 10K scenes; HSSD-200 shows cross-distribution saturation near ~10² scenes — but both entangle layout with appearance, which WanderAI's symbolic interface removes by construction. Required evidence: N-room sweep (e.g., 10/100/1000) evaluated on a fixed held-out set, plus one out-of-generator test set (hand-designed rooms, or 3D MuJoCo scenes for a 2D-trained policy), locating the knee of the curve.

**C5. Quantifying what reward-tuning adds over prompting and over frontier geometry in the small-LLM regime.** Aghaei et al. (2025) showed frozen-LLM contributions largely reduce to engineered geometry; LFG showed frozen LLM-as-planner fails. The claim: RL fine-tuning makes LLM-as-planner viable, and either beats or matches geometric FBE *without any map or planner in the policy loop* (itself a distinct claim about learned exploration under bounded symbolic memory). Required evidence: matched-protocol comparison of tuned-LLM, prompted-LLM (with LFG-style positive/negative polling as the strong prompting arm), and nearest-frontier FBE, reported as SPL/SoftSPL with CIs.

## 5. Threats to novelty

- **To C1 — VLN-R1 (Qi et al., 2025).** The single closest prior work: GRPO-style reinforcement fine-tuning of a small (2B) language-family model emitting low-level forward/turn/stop actions in a continuous 3D environment, with a 2B-beats-7B-SFT result. If reviewers read WanderAI as "VLN-R1 with a simpler task," the defense must rest on (a) demonstration-free geometric reward vs. VLN-R1's expert-trajectory alignment, (b) text-only symbolic observations vs. egocentric video, and (c) search under partial observability vs. instruction following. GLAM is the secondary threat here: it already established that online RL grounds a small LM policy over symbolic text observations of a procedurally generated world, with strong generalization claims — WanderAI must be explicit that its advance over GLAM is continuous-space embodied search, metric low-level actions, episodic critic-free GRPO, and SPL-based held-out-scene evaluation.
- **To C2 — DD-PPO / Habitat reward lineage plus ArCHer.** The reward is verbatim standard practice (Savva et al., 2019; Wijmans et al., 2020); any hint of claiming the reward itself is novel sinks the paper. ArCHer threatens the "shaping substitutes for a critic" framing: if a step-level-advantage variant (GiGPO) dominates whole-episode GRPO even with dense shaping, C2 collapses to "we used the standard reward."
- **To C3 — Ye et al. (2021) and Savinov et al. (2019).** The visit-count signal itself is theirs (reward-side and oracle-baseline respectively); MemoNav already showed memory curation improves navigation. C3 survives only as a *comparison* claim (observation-side vs. reward-side), not as inventing exploration memory.
- **To C4 — HSSD-200 and ProcTHOR.** Both own the scene-scaling question at scale and with real benchmarks. If reviewers judge that "layout-only" isolation in toy procedural rooms doesn't inform the realistic setting, C4 reduces to a sanity check; the defense is that HSSD explicitly could not disentangle layout diversity from visual fidelity, and WanderAI can.
- **To C5 — Aghaei et al. (2025, "When Engineering Outruns Intelligence").** The most dangerous single result in the corpus: if WanderAI's fine-tuned LLM fails to beat nearest-frontier FBE, this paper predicts exactly that outcome and the contribution inverts into a negative result (which should be pre-registered as an acceptable finding: "reward-tuning does not close the geometry gap at 4B scale"). LFG also partially anticipates the prompting-vs-planning framing.

Cross-cutting threat: **protocol legibility.** Without a STOP action, SoftSPL, frozen episode sets, and multi-seed statistics, none of C1–C5 is defensible regardless of novelty — the evaluation-rigor strand (Anderson et al., 2018; Henderson et al., 2018; Agarwal et al., 2021) defines the minimum bar reviewers will apply.
